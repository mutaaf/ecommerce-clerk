Platform & architecture

The commerce layer between AI intent and ecommerce transactions.

This page is for the person your merchant forwards this to. It covers the two product surfaces, the tool layer, how attribution is defined and audited, what the model is and is not allowed to do, and the shape of the first build.

Two surfaces, one integration

The merchant integrates once. We manage where it sells.

Product A is what ships now, because it is the surface we control and the fastest way to prove revenue. Product B is the same commerce engine pointed outward once the transaction loop is trusted.

Architecture

The model sits beside the commerce engine, never on top of it.

Language understanding and tool selection come from a replaceable provider. Everything that touches money, stock or state is deterministic and ours.

Positioning, stated plainly

This is not an OpenAI wrapper.

Model providers are infrastructure and they are interchangeable. The architecture has to let us change one without rewriting the commerce platform. What follows is the actual split.

Adapters

Platform agnostic, stated as a contract.

Agnostic is easy to claim and hard to prove, so here is the proof: every adapter implements the same interface, owns three concerns, and touches nothing else. Everything above the adapter is written once. That is why the second adapter cost a fraction of the first, and why an unusual stack is a conversation rather than a rebuild.

Commerce tool layer

The agent gets verbs, not access.

It never issues a query against the store. It calls a fixed set of narrowly defined tools, each one validated, scoped to a merchant, and logged with the session that invoked it.

Recommendation safety

Retrieve, then reason. Never guess.

Price, availability, variants and compatibility are transactional facts. They come from deterministic commerce APIs, not from the model's memory, which is what keeps the agent from inventing a product you do not sell.

Attribution

Every order carries a receipt for how it happened.

Attribution is not a report generated at month end. It is an ID written at the moment of intent that travels all the way into the order record.

What counts as AI attributed revenue

This definition determines the invoice, so it is written down, applied consistently, and auditable line by line in the dashboard.

Security model

The destructive operations are not exposed at all.

Not guarded by a prompt. Not discouraged by an instruction. Absent from the tool schema, so the model has no path to them regardless of what a customer types into the chat.

MVP technical shape

Deliberately small. One question to answer.

Can an AI product expert measurably drive ecommerce transactions? Everything not required to answer that is out of scope for V1.
